**1/8 — Hook**

Cursor is the best dev tool of the decade. It is also the worst reminder that 90% of my life is not code.

So I built TARS. Same density. Same speed. Same magic. For everything-not-code.

v9.3.0-beta1 ships today.

---

**2/8 — Problem**

Every "AI assistant" that is not Cursor wants your data on their cluster, your identity in their dashboard, your wallet on their plan page.

I wanted the Cursor feeling — local files, fast keys, no cloud lock — for legal, finance, health, trading, ops.

It did not exist.

---

**3/8 — TARS**

TARS is a local-first AI cockpit. The app runs on your Mac. Data lives in 9 SQLite files in ~/.tars/. Voice is real (whisper.cpp or your own OpenAI key). Every action emits a hash-chained receipt you can audit.

Billed through @meeet_world. Free tier is forever.

---

**4/8 — Cinematic cockpit**

Wave A closes the Cursor parity gap. Models switcher with per-request cost labels. MCP servers panel. Rules engine in .tars/rules.yml. Cmd+K palette v2 — 10ms across 5k entries.

First time TARS feels like a real product. Not a demo.

---

**5/8 — @mentions, but for life**

Type @ in the chat — resolver popup for @file:, @docs:, @web:, @code:, @recent:, @agent:. Tokens inline as system context on send.

The same primitive Cursor uses for code, now pointed at your taxes, contracts, charts, briefings. Receipts attach.

---

**6/8 — Consumption console**

The thing I am quietly most proud of. Every metered call emits a usage event. Soft cap at 80%, hard cap at 100%. Topup routes through the meeet.world rail.

You see, in real time, what you are spending and on what. No surprise invoice ever again.

---

**7/8 — Why this matters**

Local-first is not a niche. It is what AI was always supposed to be. Your context, your keys, your hardware, your receipts.

The cloud is a delivery channel. Not a kidnapper.

Cursor proved the UX. TARS proves the UX is portable.
